# Spike E — Synthetic likeness comparison (phase 2a, 2026-10-12)

Question 1 of Spike E (`.planning/PROJECT_SPIKES.md` §Spike E): **can we generate
consistent identity?** This run compares two treatments for putting one child's
likeness into a storybook scene. It does **not** address question 2 (independent
detection/QA) beyond recording what was measured.

Evidence: [`output/identity-comparison-2026-10-12/`](../../output/identity-comparison-2026-10-12/README.md)
and its [pose trial](../../output/identity-comparison-2026-10-12/pose-trial/README.md)
(measurements, registrations and reproduce scripts in git; images kept out of the
public repo).

## Setup

- **Reference:** one invented photorealistic child portrait (frontal, closed-mouth
  smile), generated for this test. No real child data was used.
- **A: reference-conditioned illustration.** The image model receives the reference
  as the sole identity input and paints the whole scene in a watercolour treatment.
- **B: protected-face composite.** A is the base scene; the reference face is copied
  in with manual 2D registration and a feathered ellipse (`composite.cjs`, `run.cjs`,
  `pngjs` only, no network).
- **Scenes:** frontal garden scene, three-quarter head turn, wide full-body
  ("small face"), and an open-mouth laugh.

### Result

Status:
INCONCLUSIVE. There is directional evidence for the generation approach, but no
likeness measurement, no real-child validation and no qualified provider.

Candidates tested:
A: reference-conditioned illustration (built-in image generation tool). B: local
protected-face compositor over A.

Measurements:
- B changed **0 pixels outside the registered region** in every case (frontal and all
  three pose-trial scenes, checked on the decoded saved PNGs).
- Interior pixels taken from the source face: 38,127 (frontal); 76,489 / 12,789 /
  103,096 (three-quarter / small-face / expression). Sampling is bilinear, so these
  are not byte-identical to the original pixels.
- 1 visible child per scene in all outputs.
- The "small face" head was about 13% of image height, not the requested 9%, so this
  was not a demanding small-face test.
- These are **preservation checks, not likeness scores**. No automated identity
  score, blinded human review or parent rating was collected.

Observed strengths:
- A followed the requested head turn and expression while keeping one consistent
  painted style across face, hair, clothes and scene.
- B reproduces the source facial detail exactly within its region and leaves the
  rest of the scene untouched (deterministic, auditable).

Observed failures:
- **B fails when the pose differs from the reference.** In the three-quarter scene the
  frontal face rotated in 2D gives a camera-facing gaze and the wrong head perspective,
  with a visible cheek seam.
- **B cannot change expression.** The requested laugh became the source's
  closed-mouth smile.
- B's photographic skin texture clashes with the painted hair and scene (a style seam).
  Hair and outer ears still come from A.
- A redraws facial features. Whether parents read the result as *their* child is
  unmeasured.

Operational cost:
Not measured. Images came from the chat's built-in tool, not a production
provider, so latency and per-image cost are unknown. The compositor is free and
local, but needed manual registration per scene.

Privacy/data implications:
- A sends the child's reference photo to an image provider on every page generation,
  unless that provider supports a reusable private reference. That needs a provider
  whose retention, training and deletion terms are verified (`ProviderDataPolicy`;
  AGENTS.md: no child photos to providers without supported deletion or with training
  use).
- B keeps generation reference-free, but stores the child's real face in every
  rendered page.
- Both need D017 private upload, consent and retention before any real photo is used.

Decision:
KEEP OPEN on the provider and the QA approach. Provisional direction, pending parent
validation: **A (reference-conditioned illustration with a parent-approved reference
look)** as the general path. B stays a constrained option only for pages whose pose and
expression match an available reference, and is never applied silently.

Decision rationale:
B's guarantee (exact pixels) breaks on the variations a storybook needs: turned heads,
different expressions, small faces. A handles those, at the cost of likeness that is
model-dependent and currently unmeasured. One synthetic child in four scenes can show
where B fails, but cannot show that A is good enough.

Production consequences:
- Do not replace the production illustration stand-ins on this evidence.
- The image pipeline (roadmap item 3) should be built around an **immutable,
  parent-approved appearance/reference look** (F-005 Character Bible) that every page
  job conditions on, with per-page jobs that can be regenerated in isolation.
- Likeness QA cannot be thresholded yet (Spike E rule: no invented numbers). Child
  count and one-visible-child checks can be automated now.

Follow-up:
1. **Parent review.** Show A/B for the four scenes to target parents and record a
   preference plus "looks like my child" ratings.
2. **Real provider trial.** Run A through at least one provider with a verified
   `ProviderDataPolicy` (reusable private reference preferred). Use more than one
   synthetic identity, the full degradation matrix in `spike/identity-qa`
   (pose/lighting/expression/occlusion/distance), and record latency and cost.
3. **Independent evaluator vs human review.** Plug the real evaluator and blinded
   reviews into the phase-1 harness here (`npm run experiment`) and compute agreement
   (kappa, swap-detection F1) before choosing any threshold.
4. **Then build:** D017 private upload and consent, an approved reference look, and one
   recoverable page-illustration job.
